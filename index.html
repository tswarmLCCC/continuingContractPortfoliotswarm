Project 1: Course Development (AI Program Development)

Abstract: This project details my central role in researching, designing, and launching the first Associate of Applied Science (AAS) in Artificial Intelligence in the state of Wyoming, officially initiated in Fall 2025. Recognizing a rapid shift in workforce demands, I completed market research and collaborated with our regional Professional Advisory Committee (PAC) to design a state-aligned technical pathway. I developed curriculum specifications and syllabi for seven entirely new artificial intelligence (AIML) courses, alongside a substantial modernization of Computer Science II (COSC 2030) and the creation of Data Engineering (COSC 2060). Throughout this curriculum build, I integrated Bloom’s Taxonomy to systematically scaffold student learning, transitioning learners from foundational "no-code" logic and ethics to applied machine learning and neural network deployment. I successfully navigated the multi-tier local Academic Standards Committee, the Wyoming Community College Commission (WCCC) approval process, and the Higher Learning Commission (HLC) notification cycle. Funded in part by the state's Wyoming Innovation Partnership (WIP) grant, this program positions Laramie County Community College as a regional leader in emerging technology education. The resulting curriculum provides our community with accessible, industry-aligned credentials that bridge directly to high-demand technical careers.

Project 2: Student-Centered Instruction

Abstract: This project demonstrates my student-centered approach to maximizing engagement and mastery across LCCC's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence courses. In courses like Python Programming (COSC 2409) and Computer Science I (COSC 1030), I replaced conventional weekly homework policing with cumulative "Mini Programming Portfolios." This model gives students agency over their learning, allowing them to choose their own development environments and demonstrate mastery through a student-choice evaluation: an asynchronous screen-share video explanation or a live 1-on-1 virtual code conference. Student course evaluations consistently confirm that this dialogue-first model is far more personalized and less stressful than automated syntax grading. The portfolios transform evaluation into collaborative mentorship, eliminating the exam panic that frequently causes introductory programming students to withdraw. Furthermore, student-centered instruction requires active institutional advocacy. When a Spring 2026 administrative scheduling decision canceled a required database prerequisite, I designed a modern PostgreSQL curriculum over the summer and launched it as an independent study in Fall 2026 to keep three graduating students on track without delaying their degrees. This project exhibits my dedication to meeting learners where they are and removing barriers to their academic progression.

Project 3: Promoting a Welcoming and Inclusive Environment

Abstract: This project documents my intentional strategies to make computer science and artificial intelligence accessible, supportive, and inclusive for all learners. Technical computing disciplines are historically plagued by high attrition and equity gaps, often intimidating non-traditional, female, and first-generation college students who lack prior exposure to code. To dismantle this barrier, I implemented "no-code" visual on-ramps in introductory courses like COSC 1010 and AIML 1010. By grounding initial units in logic puzzles, system flowcharts, and real-world ethical problem-solving, students discover that computational thinking is a human skill rather than an innate talent. Complementing this curricular on-ramp is an intentional, high-touch communication protocol: I maintain a strict 12-to-24-hour email response commitment and post weekly unedited video check-ins that humanize the online space. The quantitative results from Anthology Student Course Questionnaires (SCQs) validate this approach, showing consistent average scores between 3.86 and 4.00 out of 4.00 on the "welcoming and inclusive" metric across all terms. This project showcases how cultivating psychological safety, normalizing mistakes, and providing transparent support allows diverse students to thrive in rigorous STEM pathways.

Project 5: Teaching Effectiveness Project Narrative

Abstract: This project presents a comprehensive, data-driven narrative of my instructional performance, student learning outcomes, and responsive course improvements across my continuing contract review period. Drawing upon official course success metrics from LCCC Institutional Research (IR), multi-semester quantitative ratings from Anthology (Campus Labs) Student Course Questionnaires, and qualitative student feedback, I evaluate my teaching effectiveness and demonstrate continuous instructional adaptation. The data reveals consistent strengths in instructor accessibility, course organization, and technological integration, with student responsiveness ratings consistently averaging between 3.89 and 4.00 out of 4.00. However, evaluations also highlighted a critical challenge: recurring student fatigue with our interactive digital textbook (ZyBooks), which learners frequently described as dry, overly repetitive, and punitive regarding syntax formatting. This narrative details how I actively resolved this feedback by reducing required automated reading checkpoints, rebalancing grading weights toward custom, hands-on programming projects, and producing targeted video walkthroughs to demystify complex logic. The subsequent data confirms the success of these interventions, evidenced by sustained 95% pass rates in Python Programming and a dramatic drop in Computer Science I DFW rates from 31.8% to 9.1%.

The ZyBooks Evolution & Future OER Vision

In response to constructive student feedback regarding reading fatigue in ZyBooks, I implemented a 3-part curricular reform: re-weighting grading away from mechanical reading clicks, producing bite-sized video walk-throughs, and introducing verbal-defense mini portfolios. Moving forward, I am leading an initiative to migrate foundational programming courses to custom, interactive Open Educational Resources (OER), eliminating textbook costs for LCCC students.

Teaching Philosophy & Pedagogical Framework

My teaching philosophy is grounded in a simple commitment: meet students where they are, remove unnecessary friction, and scaffold rigorous skills until they achieve independent mastery.

The Three Core Pillars

1. Content-First Curriculum Architecture

Authoring all instructional materials in LMS-agnostic Markdown to ensure portability, durable version control, and seamless mapping from Course Outlines of Record to weekly milestones.

2. The Five-Step Cognitive Apprenticeship

Scaffolding complex technical topics through a repeatable loop: Real-World Problem → Tools/Syntax → Conceptual Deconstruction → Guided Live Coding ("I Do / We Do") → Independent Project ("You Do").

3. AI-Assisted Course Engineering

Utilizing generative workflows to automate practice problems, diagnostic check-ins, and study scaffolds, reclaiming faculty time for high-impact 1-on-1 student code mentoring.
