## 1. Meeting Context & Strategic Objectives

The Artificial Intelligence Advisory Group was convened to guide the curriculum build for Wyoming's first Associate of Applied Science in Artificial Intelligence, funded through the Wyoming Innovation Partnership (WIP) Phase II grant. The committee evaluated labor market demand, entry-level skill requirements, transfer pathways to the University of Wyoming, and student recruitment pipelines.

---

## 2. Key Stakeholder Findings & Direct Curricular Actions

### Finding 1: Builders vs. Users & The Vital Need for SQL (Erin Moore, Gannett Peak Technologies)
* **Stakeholder Feedback:** Advisory members emphasized that community college graduates must be trained as **creators and builders**, not merely end-users of commercial AI tools. **Erin Moore from Gannett Peak Technologies** served as an instrumental voice on the committee, emphasizing that junior technical hires rarely spend their time training foundational models from scratch; rather, **over 80% of entry-level industry workload centers on SQL querying, data ingestion, pipeline validation, and database hygiene.** She pointed out that legacy database courses teaching basic desktop tools do not prepare students for modern software environments.
* **Curricular Action Taken by Trevor Swarm:**
  * In direct response to Erin Moore and industry partners, completely rebuilt LCCC's legacy Microsoft Access class (`CMAP 1815: Database Applications`) into a modern, industry-aligned SQL and data engineering course: **`COSC 2060: Data Engineering`**. The new course replaces basic GUI desktop exercises with enterprise-grade relational database architecture, standard SQL queries, table joins, cloud ETL pipelines, and data quality assurance.
  * Embedded local AI development environments via the newly established WIP-funded **LCCC AI Lab**, allowing students to deploy and fine-tune open-weights models locally and safely.

### Finding 2: Verbal and Technical Defense in the Capstone
* **Stakeholder Feedback:** Eric Trowbridge (Blue Federal Credit Union) and regional software managers noted that technical talent frequently struggles to communicate algorithmic choices and ethical risks to non-technical executive teams.
* **Curricular Action Taken by Trevor Swarm:**
  * Structured the final capstone course (**`AIML 2395: Artificial Intelligence Capstone`**) around an authentic client problem requiring both a working technical artifact and a mandatory oral presentation/defense.
  * Extended the "Mini Programming Portfolio" model used in foundational courses into upper-division AI courses.

### Finding 3: Accessible On-Ramps for Non-Traditional Students
* **Stakeholder Feedback:** Demographic tracking revealed that approximately 50% of incoming prospective AI students possess little to no prior formal programming experience. Julie Calkins (LCSD#1) also highlighted the need to demystify AI for parents and prospective high school students.
* **Curricular Action Taken by Trevor Swarm:**
  * Designed the first semester with zero barrier-to-entry coursework: **`AIML 1010` (Introduction to AI)** and **`AIML 1020` (Ethics in AI)** use visual heuristics, conceptual reasoning, and ethical frameworks before students encounter formal syntax in **`COSC 1010`** and **`AIML 1030`**.

### Finding 4: Articulation Exploration with the University of Wyoming
* **Stakeholder Feedback:** Dr. Raya Hegeman from the UW School of Computing supported exploring 2+2 transfer pathways for LCCC students interested in four-year degrees in Applied Computing, Computer Science, or Data Science.
* **Curricular Action Taken by Trevor Swarm:**
  * Coordinated with Dr. Raya Hegeman and UW faculty to draft an initial course crosswalk (`Artificial Intelligence AS Crosswalk.xlsx`) mapping foundational computing credits.
