## Abstract

This project details my central role in researching, designing, and launching the first Associate of Applied Science (AAS) in Artificial Intelligence in the state of Wyoming, officially initiated in Fall 2025. Recognizing a rapid shift in workforce demands, I completed market research and collaborated with our regional Professional Advisory Committee (PAC) to design a state-aligned technical pathway. I developed curriculum specifications and syllabi for seven entirely new artificial intelligence (AIML) courses, alongside the creation of Data Engineering (COSC 2060) and the modernization of our computing pathways. Throughout this curriculum build, I integrated Bloom’s Taxonomy to systematically scaffold student learning, transitioning learners from foundational "no-code" logic and ethics to applied machine learning, computer vision, and neural models. I successfully navigated the multi-tier local Academic Standards Committee, the Wyoming Community College Commission (WCCC) approval process, and the Higher Learning Commission (HLC) notification cycle. Funded in direct response to the Wyoming Innovation Partnership (WIP) Phase II grant, this program positions Laramie County Community College as a regional leader in emerging technology education. The resulting curriculum provides our community with accessible, industry-aligned credentials that bridge directly to high-demand technical careers.

### Artifact 2: AIML Course Curriculum Scaffold & Official Competencies
* **Context & Nature:** Comprehensive course outlines and approved competencies for AIML 1010, AIML 1020, AIML 1030, AIML 2030, AIML 2040, AIML 2050, AIML 2395, and COSC 2060, paired with the Curriculog competency mapping.

Mapped across Curriculog on an Introduce-Reinforce-Master scaffolding continuum, these competencies transition learners from foundational 'no-code' logic in year one to applied neural network and client-facing capstone defense (`AIML 2395`) in year two.
* **Artifact Reference:** [Artifact 1.2: AIML Course Competencies & Syllabi Scaffold](artifacts/AIML_Combined_Syllabus_2025_2026.md)

### Artifact 3: AI Professional Advisory Committee (PAC) Minutes & Curricular Actions
* **Context & Nature:** Official meeting records, transcripts, and stakeholder feedback logs from PAC sessions with regional industry leaders and university partners.
* **Layman Description:** This artifact provides concrete evidence of how external feedback directly altered course designs. Regional employers and state agency partners emphasized that entry-level practitioners spend the vast majority of their time on data preparation rather than theoretical mechanics: *"Junior AI hires rarely build foundation models from scratch; over 80% of our actual technical workload is data ingestion, SQL querying, schema validation, and pipeline cleaning."* In direct response, I designed and introduced `COSC 2060: Data Engineering` to provide dedicated SQL and cloud data pipeline instruction.

## Project Reflection

### 1. Examination of Artifact Selection
These three artifacts were chosen because they trace the full lifecycle of academic program development: from external labor validation and state-level regulatory sanction (Artifact 1), down to granular classroom instructional scaffolding and continuous assessment refinement (Artifact 2), and responsive alignment through employer feedback (Artifact 3). Together, they prove that the AI program is not an isolated experiment, but a rigorous, sustainable, accredited pathway embedded within LCCC’s strategic vision.

### 2. Growth Demonstrated in Relation to Program Review
Developing a brand-new degree from scratch accelerated my professional growth as a faculty member. When I joined LCCC, my expertise centered on classroom computer science instruction. Leading this initiative required me to master multi-tier institutional governance: presenting to the Academic Standards Committee, negotiating curriculum parameters with the Wyoming Community College Commission, and collaborating closely with our Director of Assessment. Working alongside our assessment team taught me how to evolve from course-level grading to programmatic assessment design—learning to discard bloated, multi-clause outcomes in favor of clear, measurable competencies that can be tracked across multi-year degree pathways.

### 3. Key Lessons Learned
The primary lesson from this build is that curriculum design is an iterative cycle of continuous refinement, not a one-and-done administrative task. Program competencies must be clean, modular, and directly assessable. By collaborating with our assessment leadership to simplify our programmatic outcomes into four core pillars, we established clear benchmarks for capstone evaluation. Furthermore, by anchoring our program outcomes to enduring foundational competencies—algorithmic logic, ethical implications, and data pipeline fundamentals—we created a framework that can absorb new libraries and models without requiring complete programmatic overhauls.

### 4. Planned Changes and Future Steps
Moving forward, I will establish a regular two-year review cycle with our PAC to ensure our toolsets remain relevant. Additionally, I am currently collaborating with the University of Wyoming School of Computing to expand our 2+2 articulation agreements, ensuring students who wish to pursue a four-year bachelor's in computing or data science can transfer all credits seamlessly.

### 5. Broader Institutional and Community Impact
This program has transformed LCCC into Wyoming's pioneer in community college AI education. It has brought state recognition and grant funding to our division, forged relationships with global tech leaders, and, most importantly, created an accessible pathway for Wyoming residents to enter high-paying technical careers without leaving their home state.
