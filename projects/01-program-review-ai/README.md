## Abstract

This project details my central role in researching, designing, and launching the first Associate of Applied Science (AAS) in Artificial Intelligence in the state of Wyoming, officially initiated in Fall 2025. Recognizing a rapid shift in workforce demands, I completed market research and collaborated with our regional Professional Advisory Committee (PAC) to design a state-aligned technical pathway. I developed curriculum specifications and syllabi for seven entirely new artificial intelligence (AIML) courses, alongside a substantial modernization of Computer Science II (COSC 2030) and the creation of Data Engineering (COSC 2060). Throughout this curriculum build, I integrated Bloom’s Taxonomy to systematically scaffold student learning, transitioning learners from foundational "no-code" logic and ethics to applied machine learning and neural network deployment. I successfully navigated the multi-tier local Academic Standards Committee, the Wyoming Community College Commission (WCCC) approval process, and the Higher Learning Commission (HLC) notification cycle. Funded in part by the state's Wyoming Innovation Partnership (WIP) grant, this program positions Laramie County Community College as a regional leader in emerging technology education. The resulting curriculum provides our community with accessible, industry-aligned credentials that bridge directly to high-demand technical careers.

### Artifact 2: AIML 7-Course Curriculum Scaffold & Master Syllabi
* **Status:** Approved by LCCC Academic Standards Committee (Fall 2024).
* **Summary:** Syllabi and learning competencies for the seven newly created courses:
  1. `AIML 1010`: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (*No-code foundations & prompt engineering*)
  2. `AIML 1020`: Artificial Intelligence Ethics (*Societal impact, bias, and governance*)
  3. `AIML 1100`: Machine Learning Foundations (*Scikit-Learn, data preprocessing*)
  4. `AIML 2100`: Deep Learning & Neural Networks (*TensorFlow, PyTorch architectures*)
  5. `AIML 2200`: Computer Vision (*Image classification & object recognition*)
  6. `AIML 2300`: Natural Language Processing (*Transformers, language modeling*)
  7. `AIML 2900`: Applied AI Capstone (*Industry partner client projects*)

## Project Reflection

### 1. Examination of Artifact Selection
These three artifacts were curated to demonstrate the complete progression of program development: external validation and state-level approval (Artifact 1), granular course-level competency scaffolding (Artifact 2), and continuous curriculum refinement through employer partnership (Artifact 3). Together, they establish that the AI degree is not a superficial collection of electives, but an intentionally designed, accredited workforce pipeline.

### 2. Growth Demonstrated in Relation to Program Review
Leading this degree development was a transformative professional experience. When I arrived at LCCC, my focus was largely within the four walls of the classroom. Spearheading this initiative required me to develop fluency in institutional governance, state regulatory compliance, budget forecasting with grant administrators, and regional employer relations. I gained critical expertise in translating high-level workforce trends into measurable, accredited student competencies.

### 3. Key Lessons Learned
The central lesson of this project is that emerging technology curricula must be inherently modular. Because artificial intelligence evolves rapidly, locking a degree into rigid textbook chapters guarantees obsolescence. By anchoring our program to core computational thinking, ethical discernment, and data pipeline fundamentals, we built an adaptable curriculum that can absorb new libraries and AI models without requiring structural program revisions.

### 4. Planned Changes and Future Steps
Moving forward, I will maintain a bi-annual review cadence with our PAC to monitor industry shifts. Additionally, I am currently collaborating with the University of Wyoming School of Computing to finalize comprehensive 2+2 articulation agreements, ensuring that LCCC graduates can transition seamlessly into four-year baccalaureate degrees in Computer Science or Data Science.

### 5. Broader Institutional and Community Impact
This initiative has positioned Laramie County Community College as Wyoming’s pioneer in two-year artificial intelligence education. It has generated regional recognition, secured state grant investment, and opened doors for Wyoming students to enter high-paying technical careers while remaining in their local communities.
