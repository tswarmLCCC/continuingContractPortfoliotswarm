## Abstract

This project details my role in researching, designing, and launching the first Associate of Applied Science (AAS) in Artificial Intelligence in the state of Wyoming, officially initiated in Fall 2025. Drawing upon prior instructional experience teaching Computer Science and Data Science at the graduate level, I engaged in deliberate pedagogical adaptation to scaffold advanced technical concepts for community college learners. In response to regional workforce demands, I collaborated with our Professional Advisory Committee (PAC) to design a state-aligned technical pathway, authoring curriculum specifications and syllabi for seven new artificial intelligence (AIML) courses alongside Data Engineering (COSC 2060). Working closely with the Director of Assessment and academic leadership, our team structured competencies using Bloom’s Taxonomy to transition students from foundational logic and ethics to applied machine learning and neural architectures. Supported by the Wyoming Innovation Partnership (WIP) Phase II grant, the curriculum advanced through multi-level internal stakeholder reviews, external alignment discussions with the University of Wyoming, and two presentations to the LCCC Board of Trustees, followed by formal defense before state bodies by the Vice President of Academic Affairs. The resulting program establishes an accessible, industry-aligned pathway into high-demand technical careers.

## Project Reflection

### 1. Examination of Artifact Selection
These three artifacts were chosen because they trace the full lifecycle of academic program development: from external labor validation and state-level regulatory sanction (Artifact 1), down to granular classroom instructional scaffolding and continuous assessment refinement (Artifact 2), and responsive alignment through employer feedback (Artifact 3). Together, they prove that the AI program is not an isolated experiment, but a rigorous, sustainable, accredited pathway embedded within LCCC’s strategic vision.

### 2. Growth Demonstrated in Relation to Program Review
Developing a brand-new degree from inception accelerated my professional growth, particularly in bridging graduate-level rigor with community college access. Prior to joining LCCC, my instructional background centered on teaching Computer Science and Data Science at the graduate level. Transitioning to community college education required a deliberate, reflective pedagogical adaptation: I had to re-examine foundational assumptions, deconstruct complex abstractions into accessible cognitive steps, and build intentional scaffolding for students who may enter with zero prior coding experience.

Leading the curricular development of the AI degree further broadened my institutional perspective through collaborative, multi-tiered governance. Rather than operating in isolation, I collaborated closely with our Director of Assessment, the Dean of Arts and Sciences, and our Professional Advisory Committee (PAC). Together, we vetted the curriculum across multiple levels: presenting to internal stakeholder groups, integrating iterative feedback, conducting external alignment discussions with faculty at the University of Wyoming, and twice presenting to and defending our program design before the LCCC Board of Trustees. Following these campus defenses, the Vice President of Academic Affairs (VPAA) led the formal defense of the curriculum before state regulatory bodies. This collaborative process taught me how to align high-level institutional strategy with measurable classroom learning outcomes.

### 3. Key Lessons Learned
The primary lesson from this build is that curriculum design is an iterative cycle of continuous refinement, not a one-and-done administrative task. Program competencies must be clean, modular, and directly assessable. By collaborating with my Program Director and the Director of Assessment to simplify our programmatic outcomes into four core pillars, we established clear benchmarks for capstone evaluation. Furthermore, by anchoring our program outcomes to enduring foundational competencies—algorithmic logic, ethical implications, and data pipeline fundamentals—we created an agile framework that can absorb new libraries and models without requiring complete programmatic overhauls.

### 4. Planned Changes and Future Steps
Moving forward, our curriculum will undergo an important administrative and structural evolution:
* **Advisory Committee Consolidation & Pathway Transition:** While we will continue leveraging a dedicated Professional Advisory Committee, our PAC will be formally merged and combined with the **Information Technology (IT) Pathway Professional Advisory Committee**. This consolidation directly aligns with the program transitioning administratively and academically from the School of Arts and Sciences over to the IT Pathway, creating a unified advisory pipeline for technical workforce education in Cheyenne.
* **Curriculum Sustainability & AI Currency:** In a field accelerating as rapidly as artificial intelligence, curriculum sustainability requires continuous professional currency. Program evolution will rely on ongoing alignment with this unified IT advisory board, coupled with my continuous training through **Intel** and the **National Applied AI Consortium (NAAIC)**. By staying ahead of emerging paradigms—specifically Large Language Models (LLMs) and, more recently, multi-level agentic AI instruction—we ensure our students graduate with modern, competitive competencies.

### 5. Broader Institutional and Community Impact
This program has established LCCC as Wyoming's pioneer in community college AI education. Developed through inter-institutional collaboration, grant investment, and deep regional partnership, it provides Wyoming residents with an accessible, high-value pathway into emerging technology careers while remaining in their home communities.
