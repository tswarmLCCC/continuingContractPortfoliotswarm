## Abstract

This project showcases my continuous engagement with industry leaders, state working groups, and university partners to align LCCC’s technical education with emerging workforce demands. In rapidly accelerating disciplines like Artificial Intelligence and Computer Science, faculty development cannot be confined to passive conference attendance; it requires active collaboration with industry architects. To ensure our new AI degree meets employer standards, I completed rigorous technical training through Intel and Dell's "AI for the Workforce" initiative. This partnership enabled me to integrate validated curriculum modules—such as Intel’s Data-Centric AI and computer vision frameworks—directly into our AIML coursework. Simultaneously, I coordinated with faculty at the University of Wyoming's (UW) School of Computing to negotiate comprehensive 2+2 articulation agreements, establishing guaranteed credit pathways for community college graduates transitioning into four-year computational degrees. This project features curricular integration matrices, articulation correspondence, and technical training verification. Together, they demonstrate how ongoing professional development strengthens institutional partnerships, elevates instructional rigor, and equips LCCC students with competitive, high-demand workforce credentials.

## Project Reflection

### 1. Examination of Artifact Selection
These three artifacts illustrate the three essential dimensions of modern professional development in technical education: corporate industry alignment (Artifact 1), higher education transfer articulation (Artifact 2), and personal mastery of emerging technologies paired with community outreach (Artifact 3). Together, they prove that my development activities directly benefit students, academic pathways, and institutional standing.

### 2. Growth Demonstrated in Relation to Professional Development
Prior to leading the AI curriculum build, my professional learning was primarily centered on classroom pedagogical strategies. Engaging with national initiatives like Intel's AI for the Workforce expanded my horizons, requiring me to operate at the intersection of industry certification, state workforce boards, and academic accreditation. I grew in my ability to translate enterprise technical standards into scaffolded, accessible coursework suitable for first- and second-year college students.

### 3. Key Lessons Learned
The primary takeaway from collaborating with industry and university partners is that community college curriculum must remain agile and responsive. While four-year research universities often take years to adjust core degree requirements, community colleges can rapidly adapt to regional workforce shifts. Maintaining close ties with external partners ensures our faculty know which skills are becoming obsolete and which emerging tools are essential for immediate student employability.

### 4. Planned Changes and Future Steps
I plan to deepen our relationship with regional employers by inviting industry software engineers to serve as guest reviewers for our student Capstone projects. Furthermore, I will continue pursuing advanced certifications in cloud data engineering to ensure our new database and cloud courses remain state-of-the-art.

### 5. Broader Institutional and Community Impact
My professional collaborations have positioned LCCC as an active contributor to Wyoming's statewide technology ecosystem. By building seamless transfer agreements with UW and aligning with global industry standards, we ensure that LCCC students receive an education that is respected locally, regionally, and nationally.
