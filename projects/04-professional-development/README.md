## Abstract

This project showcases my continuous engagement with industry leaders, state working groups, and university partners to align LCCC’s technical education with emerging workforce demands. In rapidly accelerating disciplines like Artificial Intelligence and Computer Science, faculty development cannot be confined to passive conference attendance; it requires active collaboration with industry architects. To ensure our new AI degree meets employer standards, I completed rigorous technical training through Intel and Dell's "AI for the Workforce" initiative. This partnership enabled me to integrate validated curriculum modules—such as Intel’s Data-Centric AI and computer vision frameworks—directly into our AIML coursework. Simultaneously, I coordinated with faculty at the University of Wyoming's (UW) School of Computing to negotiate comprehensive 2+2 articulation agreements, establishing guaranteed credit pathways for community college graduates transitioning into four-year computational degrees. This project features curricular integration matrices, articulation correspondence, and technical training verification. Together, they demonstrate how ongoing professional development strengthens institutional partnerships, elevates instructional rigor, and equips LCCC students with competitive, high-demand workforce credentials.

## Project Reflection

### 1. Examination of Artifact Selection
These three artifacts were selected to demonstrate that professional development at the community college level must operate simultaneously on three planes: direct industry workforce alignment (Artifact 1), statewide higher education transfer articulation (Artifact 2), and continuous individual technical mastery (Artifact 3). Together, they prove that my professional growth directly expands opportunities for students and elevates LCCC’s institutional standing.

### 2. Growth Demonstrated in Relation to Professional Development
When I joined LCCC, my professional development focused primarily on day-to-day classroom delivery. Leading the design of the AI program required me to expand my role into a statewide educational and industry collaborator. I learned to interface with multinational corporate education leads, state workforce development boards, and university department chairs, bridging the gap between industry expectations and academic curriculum standards.

### 3. Key Lessons Learned
The primary lesson from this work is that faculty vitality in computer science depends on continuous curiosity. Because technology evolves at an exponential rate, instructors cannot rely on static graduate school knowledge. Engaging with industry partners and attending technical workshops ensures that our curriculum anticipates where the market is going, allowing our students to graduate with skills that employers actively seek.

### 4. Planned Changes and Future Steps
I plan to formalize an industry mentorship network that pairs second-year AI and Computer Science students with regional software engineers. Furthermore, I will continue pursuing advanced credentials in cloud computing (AWS/Azure) to support our expanding data engineering offerings.

### 5. Broader Institutional and Community Impact
By aligning our curriculum with Intel, Dell, and the University of Wyoming, LCCC has earned a reputation as a forward-thinking institution capable of delivering cutting-edge technical education. This partnership ecosystem ensures that Wyoming students have access to world-class training right in Cheyenne.
