## 2. Maintaining Currency in Rapidly Evolving AI Disciplines

In computer science and artificial intelligence, professional development is not a one-time compliance activity; it is an ongoing, essential refinement process for maintaining program currency. Because foundational paradigms have shifted dramatically from static machine learning models to generative Large Language Models (LLMs) and autonomous agentic workflows:
* **Self-Directed Technical Sprints:** Actively experiment with and benchmark emerging open-weights models locally within the LCCC AI Lab to test latency, hardware requirements, and pedagogical utility.
* **National Applied AI Consortium (NAAIC):** Collaborate with national community college leaders to develop standardized classroom lab activities for generative AI and agentic reasoning architectures.
* **Curricular Injection:** Rapidly translate emerging tools into modular course updates across `AIML 1010`, `AIML 2030`, `AIML 2050`, and `AIML 2395`.

## 3. Community Outreach & STEM Pipeline Building

### Discovery Day: Interactive AI Demonstrations (K-12 Outreach)
* **Context:** Annual campus-wide event welcoming hundreds of regional middle and high school students to explore college STEM programs.
* **Instructional Activity:** Delivered 20–30 five-minute interactive micro-lectures demonstrating **reinforcement learning AI trained to play video games in real time**. 
* **Pedagogical Strategy:** Tailored technical explanations dynamically to match student grade levels, using visual reward curves and simulation loops to demystify how machine learning algorithms "learn" through trial and error.
* **Pipeline Impact:** Inspired K-12 interest in LCCC's computing courses and demonstrated accessible pathways into local technology careers.
